## Recommended dataset roles

- **Primary same-place/same-overpass comparisons:** MOD14 and MYD14 Level‑2 swaths paired with the corresponding VIIRS 375 m Level‑2 swaths VNP14IMG, VJ114IMG, and VJ214IMG. These preserve swath/time context and provide fire-pixel geolocation and FRP. Match actual UTC acquisition times and locations; do not equate same calendar date with same observation time.
- **Convenient harmonized event tables:** NASA FIRMS NRT vector products. Their tabular fields include acquisition date/time and pixel-footprint `Scan`/`Track`, useful for joins and QA. They are NRT products and their field availability can vary by sensor/product; use the standard Level‑2 archive as the science reference.
- **Daily context, not precise time matching:** MOD14A1/MYD14A1 and VNP14A1/VJ114A1/VJ214A1. These are daily 1 km tiled composites. A detection may be retained without preserving its exact overpass time, so they are not the preferred source for same-time matchup analysis.

### Level‑2 swath fields and quality

**MOD14/MYD14.** The 2-D `fire mask` and `algorithm QA` layers are accompanied by sparse fire-pixel SDS arrays, including `FP line`, `FP sample`, `FP latitude`, `FP longitude`, `FP T21`, `FP T31`, `FP power`, `FP confidence`, `FP AdjCloud`, `FP AdjWater`, `FP WinSize`, and view/solar-angle fields. `FP power` is FRP in MW. `FP confidence` is 0–100%; the mask classes are low/nominal/high confidence (classes 7/8/9; guide thresholds are <30%, 30–<80%, and ≥80%). The pixel-table line/sample values identify each detection’s position within the granule. The L2 product does **not** provide FIRMS-style `Scan` and `Track` footprint-size columns as its fire-pixel fields. The granule/file time and pixel line are needed to recover finer observation-time context; do not interpret the `HHMM` in a file name as an independently stored per-pixel time. [1]

**VNP14IMG/VJ114IMG/VJ214IMG.** The 375 m swath files include 2-D `fire mask` and `algorithm QA` layers plus sparse arrays including `FP_line`, `FP_sample`, `FP_latitude`, `FP_longitude`, `FP_power`, `FP_confidence`, fire/background channel temperatures and radiances, and viewing/solar geometry. `FP_power` is FRP in MW; confidence is encoded as 7/8/9 for low/nominal/high. The user guide describes a roughly six-minute granule, about 202 scans, 32 detector rows per scan and 6,400 samples per row. `FP_line`/`FP_sample` locate the pixel in the image. The guide's listed fire-pixel arrays do not include FIRMS-style `Scan`/`Track` dimensions or a standalone `Acq_Time` field per detection; use granule timing plus line/sample or a validated FIRMS vector export for event-time fields. [3]

**Important FRP limitation:** the VIIRS 375 m FRP retrieval uses 750 m M13 information. Multiple 375 m detections within one 750 m retrieval footprint can share/split a parent FRP value. In rare saturation or difficult-background conditions, a fire detection can have zero/null FRP. Do not sum FRP without accounting for this design and QA flags. [3]

### Daily gridded products

MOD14A1/MYD14A1 provide `FireMask`, `QA`, `MaxFRP`, and `sample` on a 1 km sinusoidal tile grid. Their files nominally contain up to eight daily planes; `Dates` metadata associates planes with dates. `MaxFRP` is the daily maximum FRP per grid cell (scaled by 0.1 MW). `sample` records the original scan sample for fire-class cells, but a daily gridded cell does not preserve a full overpass-time series. Some files contain fewer than eight planes when acquisition data are missing. [1]

VIIRS A1 products likewise provide daily 1 km tiled composites rather than native 375 m event records. The VIIRS guide documents `FireMask`, `MaxFRP`, `QA`, and `sample` variables. Daily compositing, reprojection, and downscaling to a 1 km grid mean these products should not be treated as direct substitutes for the native swath products for exact matchup work. [3]

## NASA FIRMS near-real-time vector datasets

FIRMS distributes NRT active-fire observations in GIS/table-friendly forms and publishes links for these products through NASA Earthdata and FIRMS. Its NRT data are generally available within about three hours of observation on a best-effort basis; FIRMS states that the last 24 hours, 48 hours, and 7 days of SHP/KML/CSV files are refreshed hourly, while older data are available through the archive download tool. ## Matchup cautions and lifecycle note

MODIS and VIIRS do not observe every location at the same instant. Use each observation’s UTC time, geolocation, platform and pixel footprint, and set a temporal/spatial matching rule appropriate to the analysis. This inventory does **not** assume a match window, confidence cutoff, or a common FRP calibration. Pixel-center coordinates identify the detection pixel, not necessarily the combustion point. Clouds, scan-edge pixel growth, saturation, viewing geometry, and differing detection algorithms affect apparent agreement. [1] [3] [5] [6]

NASA’s alert, last updated 14 October 2026, says delivery of new Suomi-NPP science data is scheduled to cease at **13:00 UTC on 2 November 2026**, with the date subject to change, and advises transition to NOAA‑20/NOAA‑21 alternatives. This affects future continuity planning for `VNP14IMG`/`VNP14A1` and S‑NPP FIRMS streams; it does not mean their archived historical records disappear. [8]
